## Understanding Ketamine: Medical and Recreational Contexts

Ketamine, a dissociative anesthetic, has been used medically since the 1960s for pain relief, depression treatment, and veterinary purposes. In Canada, it's Schedule I under federal law, meaning it's tightly controlled. Legitimate access requires a prescription from a licensed physician, often for conditions like treatment-resistant depression via clinics offering ketamine infusion therapy.

Recreational use, however, remains a concern. While some seek it for its hallucinogenic effects, risks include addiction, bladder damage (known as \ketamine cystitis\ and psychological dependence. Health Canada reports increasing misuse, with emergency room visits rising in recent years.

### Legal Framework for Ketamine in Canada

Canada's drug laws are enforced by the RCMP and Health Canada. Possession, distribution, or importation without authorization can result in fines up to $100,000 or imprisonment for up to 7 years. In 2026, enhanced border controls and digital surveillance have made illicit trade riskier.

For medical use, options include:
- **Prescription Routes:** Through psychiatrists or anesthesiologists.
- **Clinical Trials:** Ongoing studies for PTSD and chronic pain.
- **Veterinary Supply:** Strictly for animals, not human use.

Illicit sourcing is dangerous, often involving contaminated products that pose health hazards.

## FAQ

### Is ketamine legal in Canada?
Ketamine is legal only with a prescription for medical use. Recreational possession is illegal.

### How can I access ketamine legally?
Consult a doctor specializing in mental health. Clinics in major cities like Toronto and Vancouver offer supervised treatments.

### What are the health risks of ketamine?
Short-term: Nausea, dissociation. Long-term: Urinary issues, cognitive impairment. Seek professional help if dependent.

### Are there alternatives to ketamine for depression?
Yes, including SSRIs, psychotherapy, and emerging treatments like MDMA-assisted therapy in trials.
